MacPulse device batteries

The Battery tab and the Batteries panel in the menu show your Mac, Bluetooth and Logitech devices and paired mobile devices. Click a device to open its history. Device settings let you edit the name, order, favorites, hiding and alerts.

Mac and Bluetooth

The Mac battery comes from the existing system sampler. Accessory batteries are read from the macOS Bluetooth record; when the system last updated the sensor is unknown. Offline values are shown with the date of the last reading. Logitech MX devices are read over HID++; when needed, Device settings can ask for Input Monitoring permission. MacPulse never asks for this permission on its own.

iPhone, iPad, Watch and Pencil

Only devices that already trust this Mac are read. Over USB, unlock the device and trust the Mac. Over Wi-Fi, an existing wireless pairing and the same network are required; MacPulse doesn’t change pairing or Finder settings. Apple Watch is read through the iPhone’s companion data, and Pencil through the battery row of the connected iPad. Local libimobiledevice tools are used as an extra source when present. If no Pencil row arrives, its percentage stays unknown.

Watts and freshness

Mobile watts are the net flow on the battery side, calculated from voltage × current; they are not adapter consumption. Watts aren’t shown for unsupported, stale, offline or inconsistent charge-direction readings. “≈” marks an approximate value.

History and alerts

Charts cover 6 hours, 24 hours and 7 days, with automatic/full scale and point selection. Gaps in the source are not joined with a line. Notification permission is requested only from a button you press. Low, critical and full alerts follow threshold and hysteresis rules.

Local data

PilBar records are copied once on first launch; the source app and its files are left untouched. MacPulse keeps device history in its own private folder. Clearing the old device list can be undone; history is kept.
